### Part II: Investigation 4-Part Narrative (Slides 5–44)

#### OBS_001: Centralized Traffic Signal Routing Anomaly
- **Slide 5 (Slide A — Observation):** Head Office absorbs 95.0% of all traffic signal complaints (403/424); field wards average only 1.4 complaints each.
- **Slide 6 (Slide B — Historical Pattern):** Consistent monthly baseline (20–35 tickets) with monsoon peaks; resolution duration averages 23.2 days.
- **Slide 7 (Slide C — Reason Investigation):** 81.6% of tickets carry verified street-level intersection coordinates; centralized digital portal routing model confirmed.
- **Slide 8 (Slide D — Conclusion & Action):** Administrative routing artifact; implement geographic dual-tagging and mobile contractor dispatch.

#### OBS_002: Speed Breaker Suburban Dominance (50.6% of All Traffic Issues)
- **Slide 9 (Slide A — Observation):** Speed breakers account for 1,061 of 2,096 traffic complaints (50.62%), monopolizing 75%–85% of suburban traffic issues.
- **Slide 10 (Slide B — Historical Pattern):** Bimodal annual peaks in Monsoon (Jul: 105, Aug: 112) and Pre-Budget Inspections (Feb: 119); +11.2% YoY growth.
- **Slide 11 (Slide C — Reason Investigation):** 58.4% request new humps for pedestrian safety; 26.8% report broken humps; 23.8% rejected under IRC:99 transit rules.
- **Slide 12 (Slide D — Conclusion & Action):** Suburban arterial expansion without pedestrian-safe geometry; replace ad-hoc humps with IRC thermoplastic rumble strips and raised table-top crossings.

#### OBS_003: Sinhgad Road Pothole Crisis (1.65× Burden Ratio)
- **Slide 13 (Slide A — Observation):** Direct pothole burden ratio reaches 1.65 (65% above city baseline); 510 potholes concentrated along a single corridor axis.
- **Slide 14 (Slide B — Historical Pattern):** Extreme monsoon dependency (78.8% in Jun–Sep; $p = 0.0005$); peak of 87 potholes in August 2025.
- **Slide 15 (Slide C — Reason Investigation):** Hotspots align with flyover pier construction at Dhayari (383), Narhe (374), and Vadgaon (175); unreinforced detour lanes fail under heavy multi-axle freight.
- **Slide 16 (Slide D — Conclusion & Action):** Construction detour failure; mandate high-grade precast/concrete detour paving in major flyover contracts.

#### OBS_004: The Drainage-Pothole Nexus in Nagar Road-Wagholi
- **Slide 17 (Slide A — Observation):** 1,317 choked drains vs 703 potholes (1.87:1 ratio); highest drainage failure volume in Pune.
- **Slide 18 (Slide B — Historical Pattern):** Synchronous co-movement ($r = 0.391$) with peak lag at 0 and +1 month; monsoon accounts for 41.9% of drain complaints and 64.3% of potholes.
- **Slide 19 (Slide C — Reason Investigation):** Spatial co-occurrence along Nagar Road, Kharadi, and Porwal Road; 80.3% drain resolution vs 52.2% pothole resolution reveals standing water impediment.
- **Slide 20 (Slide D — Conclusion & Action):** Subterranean drainage failure physically induces surface cratering; mandate joint drainage-road certification before paving.

#### OBS_005: Dhole Patil Road Administrative Backlog (54.4% Resolution Rate)
- **Slide 21 (Slide A — Observation):** Lowest resolution rate in Pune (54.40%); 37.4% open backlog ($n = 1,601$) triples the municipal baseline (10.71%).
- **Slide 22 (Slide B — Historical Pattern):** Continuous monthly deficit; median open age reaches 146 days; 156 tickets stagnant $>6$ months.
- **Slide 23 (Slide C — Reason Investigation):** 1,341 complaints (31.4% of ward total) stranded unassigned at "Reception Dholepatil A Reception"; Cantonment/Railway overlap accounts for only 3.4%.
- **Slide 24 (Slide D — Conclusion & Action):** Administrative failure of digital ticket dispatch; deploy 48-hour auto-escalation rules and a 10-day emergency triage squad.

#### OBS_006: Kondhwa-Undri Acute Infrastructure Deficit (1.41× Overall Burden)
- **Slide 25 (Slide A — Observation):** Highest overall pothole burden (1.41), highest direct pothole burden (1.51), and highest drainage burden (1.50) in Pune.
- **Slide 26 (Slide B — Historical Pattern):** 56.5% of drainage complaints occur outside the monsoon, proving chronic structural conduit undersizing.
- **Slide 27 (Slide C — Reason Investigation):** Katraj hill range runoff funnels down steep slopes into dense peri-urban settlements lacking trunk stormwater culverts.
- **Slide 28 (Slide D — Conclusion & Action):** Topographic runoff compounding peri-urban infrastructure lag; construct high-capacity interceptor drains along Katraj-Kondhwa Bypass.

#### OBS_007: The February Civic Inspection Surge vs Monsoon Peak
- **Slide 29 (Slide A — Observation):** Traffic grievances reach annual peak in February 2026 (188 tickets), exceeding the peak monsoon month of July (180).
- **Slide 30 (Slide B — Historical Pattern):** Bimodal annual distribution; July driven by weather damage (2,479 potholes), February driven by planning proposals (resurfacing: 410; speed breakers: 119).
- **Slide 31 (Slide C — Reason Investigation):** Synchronized with PMC pre-budget hearings and fiscal year-end contractor billing milestones.
- **Slide 32 (Slide D — Conclusion & Action):** Institutional budget cycle phenomenon; establish a dedicated participatory budgeting portal in Q3.

#### OBS_008: Year-over-Year Road Defect Recurrence
- **Slide 33 (Slide A — Observation):** August 2026 traffic complaints (169) exceed August 2025 (152); peripheral wards experience worsening defect rates.
- **Slide 34 (Slide B — Historical Pattern):** Central white-topped wards improve by 15–25%, while peripheral wards (Hadapsar +14.2%, Wagholi +9.8%) worsen.
- **Slide 35 (Slide C — Reason Investigation):** Temporary cold-mix asphalt patches wash away within 14 days under monsoon rain; citizen demand shifts to full resurfacing (6,401 tickets).
- **Slide 36 (Slide D — Conclusion & Action):** Cosmetic patching cycle provides zero asset longevity; enforce a public 3-year Defect Liability Period (DLP) and wet-weather mastic asphalt.

#### OBS_009: Aundh-Baner High Rejection Anomaly (17.53% Rejection Rate)
- **Slide 37 (Slide A — Observation):** Rejection rate reaches 17.53% (2,579 tickets), nearly double the city baseline (9.16%).
- **Slide 38 (Slide B — Historical Pattern):** Chronic monthly rejection rates between 14% and 21% across 14,713 total ward complaints.
- **Slide 39 (Slide C — Reason Investigation):** 25.9% of rejections cite private housing societies/internal layout roads; officer remarks reveal repeated NHAI and PMRDA jurisdictional disclaimers.
- **Slide 40 (Slide D — Conclusion & Action):** Jurisdictional mismatch in dense gated community zones; deploy GIS-enabled portal filtering to guide citizens before ticket submission.

#### OBS_010: Kasba Peth Historic Core Backlog (22.97% Pending Rate)
- **Slide 41 (Slide A — Observation):** Highest pending rate among established wards (22.97%, 782 open tickets); resolution rate lags at 70.04%.
- **Slide 42 (Slide B — Historical Pattern):** Low intake (~230/mo) but chronic stagnation; median age of open tickets exceeds 140 days.
- **Slide 43 (Slide C — Reason Investigation):** 17th-century narrow gallis ($<4–6\text{m}$) block standard municipal jetting machines and asphalt rollers; zero contractor bids for mechanized repair.
- **Slide 44 (Slide D — Conclusion & Action):** Spatial incompatibility between modern motorized repair equipment and historic old-city geometry; procure compact electric maintenance carts.
